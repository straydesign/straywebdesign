## The easiest customer to lose

There is one visitor worth more than all the rest, and most sites treat them the same as everyone else. This is the person who already decided. A friend named you, or they searched you on purpose, or they read one page and thought, fine, this is the place. They are not shopping anymore. They want to hand you the job.

Everything standing between that decision and their thumb is friction you put there. And this is the cruel part: the ready-to-buy visitor is the least patient one. They already spent their deciding energy. They have none left for a hunt.

## Where they leak out

Watch what a decided customer actually has to do on most small sites.

They want to call, so they look for the number. It is in the footer, in gray, and it is not a link, so on a phone they have to memorize it, leave the site, open the keypad, and type it from memory. Half of them fat-finger a digit and give up.

They want to know if you are open right now, and the hours are three scrolls down inside a paragraph, written as "Mon-Fri 9-5" with no mention of the holiday they are calling on.

They want to book, and the button says "Learn More," which is the opposite of what they want. They already learned enough. There is no button that says the thing they came to do.

None of these is a disaster on its own. Together they are a slow leak, and the customers it costs you are exactly the ones you already won.

## One action, made obvious

A decided customer needs one thing on every screen: the clearest possible way to do the thing they came to do.

If your business runs on phone calls, the number is a tappable link, it is near the top, and it is styled like it matters. It does not sit in the footer apologizing for itself. If it runs on bookings, the button says "Book a table" or "Request a quote," the actual words for the actual action, and it follows them down the page so it is never more than a thumb-reach away.

Pick the one action that matters most and make it loud. A page with five equal buttons has no primary action, which means the visitor has to decide again, and you already used up their deciding.

## Make the small print findable

The decided customer still has two or three real questions, and if they cannot answer them fast, the decision quietly comes undone.

Are you open now. Where exactly are you, and is there parking. Do you take the thing they have, whether that is a reservation, an insurance, a trade-in, a specific service. These are not fine print. For a ready buyer they are the last checks before they commit, and every one you hide is a reason to close the tab and call the competitor whose site answered on sight.

## Check your own site tonight

You do not need a report for this. You need your own phone.

Open your site on it, the way a customer would, and try to do the one thing your business depends on. Tap the phone number and see if it dials. Find your hours in under five seconds. Look for the button that says what you actually want people to do, and notice if it exists at all.

If any of that made you work, it made your best customer work too. The difference is you pushed through because it is your business. They had no reason to, and no one tells you when they leave.
